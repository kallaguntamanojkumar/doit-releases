Privacy policy

Last updated 27 October 2026

doIT is a desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux that builds and runs your applications in your own AWS account. This page says what doIT collects, why, who else handles it, and what you can do about it. Questions: doit4u.support@gmail.com.

In short

  • doIT runs on your computer. Your AWS keys, code and AWS resources stay with you, and doIT never collects them.
  • To license the app, doIT's licensing service keeps your email address, your name, details of the computers you use doIT on, your orders and your support requests.
  • doIT has no analytics or advertising, and never sells or rents your data.

The machine ID is hashed with a salt on your computer, and only the hash is sent. Licence keys and the secrets doIT gives each computer are stored only as fingerprints, never as they are.

Signing in with Google

When you sign in with Google, doIT asks Google only for your basic profile: your email address, whether Google has verified it, and your name. doIT uses them to find your licence, to keep you signed in on that computer, and to greet you by name in its emails. You type your password only on Google's own page, so doIT never sees it. doIT does not read your Gmail, contacts, files or any other Google data.

doIT's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What doIT never collects

  • Your AWS access keys. They stay in the AWS credentials file on your computer, and requests to AWS are signed there.
  • Your source code, project names, environment variables, commands or AWS resources.
  • Card numbers or bank logins. You pay by UPI from your own app, straight to doIT's bank account.

One exception is yours to make: a support request can include a failed run's log, which can name AWS resources. It is sent only if you tick that box, after you have seen exactly what will be sent.

Who else handles your data

  • Amazon Web Services runs doIT's licensing service, in its Sydney region, and sends doIT's emails through Amazon SES.
  • Google handles sign-in, and doIT's support inbox is a Gmail account.
  • Your bank and UPI app handle your payment. doIT's bank statement shows the payment, which can include the payer's name and UPI ID.
  • GitHub hosts this website and doIT's installers. When doIT checks for updates, it downloads a small file from GitHub, which carries nothing about you or your licence.
  • HashiCorp provides Terraform, which the app downloads from HashiCorp on its first run.
  • YouTube plays the video on this site, from its privacy-enhanced player, only after you click play.

Like any web server, GitHub, YouTube and AWS can see the network address you connect from. doIT shares your data with no one else, unless the law requires it.

How long doIT keeps it

  • The licensing service's logs are kept for 7 days, and never contain what you sent: no keys, emails, UTRs or sign-in tokens.
  • The counts that stop floods of requests are deleted after two days.
  • Everything else is kept while you have a licence, and afterwards only as long as the law requires for payment records.

The licensing service's data is encrypted at rest, and only doIT's owner can reach it.

Your choices

  • See, correct or delete your data: email doit4u.support@gmail.com from the address on your licence. On request, doIT deletes your licence, computers and support requests, and keeps only the payment records the law requires.
  • Free a computer: ask Help & support in the app, or email the address above.
  • Turn off update checks: in the app, under Help & support, Updates.
  • Remove doIT from your computer: uninstall it, then delete its data folder: ~/Library/Application Support/doIT on macOS, %APPDATA%\doIT on Windows, or ~/.config/doIT on Linux. This does not touch anything doIT built in your AWS account.

Children

doIT is made for people who run AWS accounts, and is not meant for children.

Changes

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes. If a change affects what doIT collects, licence holders are told by email first.
